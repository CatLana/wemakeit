# Images to source

A shopping list for photos the site needs. For each one: the exact filename to
save it as, where it goes, and what to look for. Drop finished files straight
into `public/images/` using these names and say so, I'll wire each one into
the right place myself.

Style note that applies to everything below: the photos already on `/about`
(the handshake photo aside) read as genuine because they're actual phone
photos, not polished stock. The two photos currently on `/book`
(`frustrated-developer.jpg`, `technical-consultation-call.jpg`) are the
opposite, generic stock photography of people who don't appear anywhere else
on the site. Where possible, favour a real, slightly imperfect photo over a
polished one, it'll read as more trustworthy and fits the humour angle better
too.

## 1. `/book` before/after pair

Replaces the current three separate sections (pain-point photo, abstract
shield icon, "guide" photo) with a single before/after visual, shown as two
images side by side.

The two photos need to read as **one story**: same person (or a visually
consistent stand-in), same rough setting, so the only thing that visibly
changes between them is their state. That contrast is what does the work,
no paragraph of copy needed underneath.

### `book-before.jpg`
**Used for:** left half of the before/after pair.
**What to look for:** someone visibly out of their depth with something
they built themselves (an AI-built app, a DIY website), not "sad," more
"I did not expect this." Genuinely useful cues: several browser tabs open,
an error message visible on screen, a half-eaten lunch, general desk
clutter. A slightly candid, unposed framing works better than a straight
eyes-to-camera shot.

### `book-after.jpg`
**Used for:** right half of the before/after pair.
**What to look for:** the same person (or a visual match, same clothing
style, same desk if possible), now on a call or in conversation, relaxed
posture, one clear thing on screen instead of chaos. Doesn't need to be
posed, a genuine mid-conversation moment (hand gesture, slight smile) sells
it better than a smiling-at-camera shot.

## 2. Custom 404 page

The site has no 404 page yet, first blank spot worth a bit of personality
instead of a default error screen.

### `404-page-not-found.jpg`
**Used for:** the new custom 404 page.
**What to look for:** something with a "we've lost something too" joke
built in, in the office/workspace, not a generic broken-robot or
tumbleweed cliché. A few directions that could work: someone visibly
searching under a desk or through a drawer, a "page not found" sticky
note held up on camera, an empty chair with a coffee still steaming (as if
whoever was meant to build this page just stepped out). Keep it a real
photo of your own space rather than a stock illustration, same reasoning
as above, it's funnier when it's obviously you.
